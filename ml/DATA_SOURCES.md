## Possible public-sector source material (not yet added to any dataset)

Public materials from anti-corruption, prosecution, audit, procurement, and court institutions may help us understand how alleged conduct is documented in Kenya. They are **candidate sources to assess**, not an approved or collected training corpus. We have not downloaded, bulk-scraped, or added source documents or case text from these institutions. A webpage being publicly readable does not by itself establish permission to reproduce its contents in a dataset or that the material is de-identified.

Any future external-source collection must begin with a source-by-source review: record the exact URL/document and access date; establish the applicable licence, reuse conditions, and any restrictions; obtain supervisor/institutional and data-protection approval where required; assess personal-data and re-identification risks; and minimize or exclude identifying text. Maintain provenance and source-domain metadata locally. Do not bulk scrape or copy documents before these checks.

### Verified access constraint for Kenya Law

The current [Kenya Law terms of use](https://new.kenyalaw.org/terms-of-use/) were checked on 2026-11-03. They prohibit scraping and bulk downloading, direct bulk-access requests to Kenya Law, and explicitly prohibit machine-learning/AI training without an explicit licensing agreement from the National Council for Law Reporting (NCLR). The terms also describe some raw legal materials as reusable under CC BY-NC 4.0, but that general license does not override the explicit ML-training restriction or additional conditions for particular collections. Therefore, do not scrape, bulk-download, or train on Kenya Law content unless NCLR has first granted explicit written permission covering the exact collection and intended ML use. A public judgment is not automatically safe or suitable training data.

As of the source review for this project, an equivalent EACC case-text reuse licence or bulk-access permission was not verified. EACC pages may identify subjects and describe allegations or proceedings. Seek written source-specific permission or terms before collecting or retaining those texts. Do not infer permission from public accessibility.

If a source passes those checks, start with a **separate external corpus**, not a silent merge into Kenya Watch report data. Two qualified reviewers should independently label the alleged-conduct text using the same versioned guide, without seeing source outcome labels during annotation; disagreements require documented adjudication. Keep source identity, publication date, document/case grouping, and the basis for any label. Prevent the same underlying case from crossing train/test partitions. Evaluate on source-separated and time-separated groups, report internal-report and external-source results separately, and only consider a combined training experiment after measuring domain differences and reviewing errors. Court outcome, charge, agency classification, or audit finding must not be substituted automatically for a human category label.

These rules mean agency/court materials **could** supplement the project after review, but they are not presently part of its training data. Do not store external case narratives in the application's operational MongoDB (`kenya_watch`); once approved, store a minimized, access-controlled external corpus in a dedicated research location with provenance and a documented retention policy. The synthetic seed corpus remains fictional, and the only intended primary source for the future report classifier remains eligible, independently reviewed Kenya Watch reports.

Request templates and an approval workflow are in [DATA_ACCESS_REQUESTS.md](DATA_ACCESS_REQUESTS.md). They are preparation only; no request has been sent and no approval has been granted.

## Applying the lecture methods to this project

The current target is **supervised, multi-class text classification**: suggest one of the established categories from the alleged-conduct narrative for a human reviewer. The first experiment is deliberately limited to two established sparse-text baselines—word/phrase TF-IDF with balanced logistic regression and word-plus-character TF-IDF with balanced linear SVM—compared with a fold-local majority-class baseline. This is a category suggestion, not a finding that an allegation is true.

The lecture classification workflow informs this experiment:

1. Audit target quality, class counts, missing text, exact duplicates, and possible incident overlap before modeling. Do not impute missing narratives or labels, and do not automatically discard rare or long reports as outliers.
2. Keep incident-linked reports in the same validation group. Fit vocabulary and model only on each training fold; this prevents related cases and corpus statistics leaking into evaluation. Scikit-learn notes that `StratifiedGroupKFold` attempts to preserve class proportions while keeping groups disjoint, but stratification is an engineering aid and cannot guarantee balanced folds in every grouped dataset.
3. Compare pre-declared text baselines against a simple majority baseline. The current workflow compares word TF-IDF + logistic regression against combined word/character TF-IDF + linear SVM, selects between them only inside grouped inner folds, and uses outer grouped folds to estimate that selection process. Report per-category precision, recall, F1, balanced accuracy, a confusion matrix, and aggregate results; accuracy alone can hide failure on rare classes. If the model is not reliably better than baseline on the agreed, important class-level measures, stop rather than tuning until a desired score appears.
4. Decide the operational trade-off between false category suggestions and missed categories with reviewers before looking at final results. Use error analysis on appropriately protected examples, and check language and region only where group sample sizes protect privacy and support meaningful estimates.
5. The experimental script reports five outer-fold macro-F1 scores and their descriptive spread, but these folds are not independent statistical confidence intervals. Freeze a later-time, incident-separated holdout before repeated model choices. Any model selection or hyperparameter tuning must happen inside training folds. Report uncertainty and limits; do not call the model successful merely because a small test split scores well.
6. Treat imbalance methods (class weights or train-fold-only resampling) as candidates to compare, not automatic fixes. Synthetic oversampling can invent implausible narrative text; it is not in the current pipeline.

Scikit-learn references used for this methodology:

- [Cross-validation and overfitting](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_validation.html): model assessment must use held-out data; model selection and final testing serve different purposes.
- [`StratifiedGroupKFold`](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.StratifiedGroupKFold.html): groups remain non-overlapping while class proportions are approximated; exact stratification is not guaranteed.
- [Classification metrics](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/model_evaluation.html#classification-metrics): macro averages weight classes equally; weighted averages reflect class prevalence; balanced accuracy helps expose majority-class inflation.
- [Sparse text classification example](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/text/plot_document_classification_20newsgroups.html): TF-IDF sparse text representations and linear classifiers are established baselines for document classification.
- [Probability calibration](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html): predicted probabilities may not equal real-world correctness rates. The current workflow does not present the model's scores as calibrated confidence or use them to automate decisions.

Regression is not the right first task because the current target is a category, not a continuous quantity. Predicting urgency or time-to-resolution would require a defensible target definition, reliable outcome timestamps, enough completed cases, and analysis of censoring and workflow bias. Do not infer guilt, corruption severity, or urgency from a regression target without an approved rationale.

K-means has no known target and would discover groups based on representation and distance. It may later help explore themes in a sufficiently large, privacy-reviewed corpus, but it cannot establish the correct allegation category and may cluster on language, county, or writing style instead of conduct. Association rules are designed for transaction baskets (the notebook's e-commerce orders); they are not a substitute for report classification. Both should remain separate exploratory research, not part of this first classifier.

## Data collection plan

1. Agree with the supervisor, reviewers, and data-protection lead on the prediction question, lawful use, access controls, retention, de-identification, and non-use for enforcement decisions.
2. Use the blinded annotation workflow on narratives: two different trained reviewers independently label from the [pilot guide](LABELING_GUIDE.md); disagreements go to documented Admin adjudication. Record concise, identity-free rationale where useful. Insufficient-information decisions are recorded separately and never converted to a category target.
3. Create a local allowlist of report IDs approved for research use and use the local-only exporter to write only finalized category labels into ignored `ml/data/raw/`. The tool exports no author identities, tracking references, or evidence, and assigns keyed group IDs from linked-case components. It does **not** anonymize narrative content. Exclude names, account IDs, tracking references, contact details, evidence, and unnecessary demographic/precise location fields through careful manual review before preparing any training input.
4. Keep the reviewed, minimized training CSV only in ignored `ml/data/approved/`. Audit and manually review duplicate/near-duplicate incidents, label definition issues, class balance, language variation, and suspicious shortcuts without printing case text in routine reports.
5. Set sample-size and acceptance criteria before training. The existing 20-per-category/five-group floor only makes the current five-fold code runnable; it is not a scientifically adequate success threshold. Increase collection until class-level estimates and their uncertainty are useful, including a later-time holdout.
6. Keep every experiment reproducible: record taxonomy version, dataset version/hash (not its contents), code version, split method, model settings, per-class metrics, subgroup suppression, limitations, and reviewer feedback. Keep data, model files, and row-level error analyses local and access-controlled.

Until these gates are met, the project has a training workflow to test methodology, not a trained or validated operational model.
